What this list still leaves out — and why

A watch order is only honest if it tells you what it dropped. These are deliberate omissions, not oversights.

Deliberately not listed as entries

Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D. — and the rest of the Marvel Television era

This is the big one, so it gets a straight answer rather than a one-liner. Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D. ran seven seasons and 136 episodes — roughly 100 hours. Its first three seasons are tightly interlocked with the films: the S.H.I.E.L.D. collapse in The Winter Soldier lands mid-season and reshapes the show, and the Sokovia Accords ripple through it. After that it drifts into its own thing — Ghost Rider, the Framework, space, time travel — and Marvel Studios has never confirmed or denied whether any of it still counts.

It is not on the numbered list because a hundred hours is too large a bet on a payoff that may never come, and because Doomsday does not depend on a single frame of it. But if you want to watch it, watch it — Seasons 1 through 3 are genuinely rewarding alongside Phase 2, and Season 4's Ghost Rider arc (Robbie Reyes, played by Gabriel Luna) is the best live-action Ghost Rider anyone has made. Slot it in after Captain America: The Winter Soldier and run it in parallel.

Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D. is now the only live-action Marvel Television show this site does not track. Everything else from that era — Agent Carter, Luke Cage, Iron Fist, The Defenders, Runaways, Cloak & Dagger, Inhumans and Helstrom — is listed in Extension D, tagged Optional, with an honest line on whether it is worth your time. None of it is required for Doomsday.

How this list was built

Every judgement call, stated plainly, so you can disagree with a specific one instead of the whole thing.

Why release order by default?

Because that's the order these stories were written to land in. Reveals are timed to it — Bucky's return, Thanos's arrival, the multiverse cracking open. Chronological order spoils several of them by construction, and a number of entries genuinely have no place on any single calendar.

Timeline order is there for a second pass. Use the toggle above the list.

Why is the whole Fox X-Men catalogue here now, not just the highlights?

Because Doomsday's announced cast reaches back into it, and once you're watching four of them you may as well know what the others contain. All seventeen are listed — thirteen films, two live-action series and the animated revival — each tiered honestly.

If you only want the ones that carry weight into Doomsday, hit "Essentials only" or read the tier on each row. Dark Phoenix and The New Mutants are on the list for completeness, and both are tagged Optional for a reason.

Why are Blade, Ghost Rider and the old Spider-Man films in the core list?

Spider-Man: No Way Home made the Raimi and Webb films canonical universes on screen, so they earn their place — and watching them makes that film land completely differently. They are tagged Recommended.

Blade and Ghost Rider are a softer call, tagged Optional. Blade is on the list because Marvel has an MCU Blade in development and because the 1998 film is the reason modern comic-book cinema exists at all. Ghost Rider is there because a new Ghost Rider film with Ryan Gosling has been announced for 2028. Neither is homework for Doomsday — they're context, and both blocks are short.

What is "Avengers: Endgame Encore"?

A theatrical re-release of Avengers: Endgame on 25 September 2026, recut with several minutes of new footage that lead directly into Doomsday. It is the last entry on the core list on purpose — it is designed to be the final thing you watch before the cinema.

If you cannot get to a screening, the original Endgame at entry 23 still covers everything the plot requires. The new footage is a bridge, not a prerequisite.

Why are Sony's films and the Spider-Verse an "extension" instead of entries?

Because they aren't the same universe and never have been. Venom brushed the MCU twice in post-credits gags and that is the entire connection; Morbius, Madame Web and Kraven have none at all. The animated Spider-Verse films are a separate continuity again — brilliant, award-winning, and completely unrelated to Doomsday's mechanics.

They are listed in full with their own progress tracking because plenty of people want to watch them, and pretending they don't exist would be its own kind of dishonest. They just don't dilute the core percentage.

What about the Marvel One-Shots?

Five short films, four to fifteen minutes each, released on Phase 1 and 2 home media. They're in the extensions block with a note on exactly where each one slots into the main list. Total runtime is under an hour for all five, and "All Hail the King" in particular is the one that Shang-Chi later picked up.

What is a "stop point"?

Some entries have to be interrupted. Black Widow's post-credits scene happens years after its film. Hawkeye's finale spoils No Way Home. When an entry needs splitting, there's a red box telling you exactly where to pause and what to come back for.

What do Essential, Recommended and Optional mean?

Essential — Doomsday is likely to be actively confusing without it. Recommended — a character or thread from it is expected to appear. Optional — it fills in texture and connective tissue, and you'd miss a reference or two without it, nothing more.

These are my judgement, not Marvel's. "Essentials only" cuts the list to the shortest defensible version.

Are the runtimes exact?

Film runtimes are theatrical cuts and accurate to the minute. Series totals are episode count multiplied by a typical episode length, so they're approximate — usually within a few percent. Titles not yet released carry estimated runtimes and are marked "Not out yet".

Where is my progress stored?

In this browser's local storage, under a single key, tracked per title rather than by position — so adding new entries to this list will never scramble what you've already ticked. It never leaves your device.

Clearing site data, using private browsing, or switching browser or device will lose it. "Copy progress" puts a plain-text summary on your clipboard if you want to move it or share it.

Install it as an app

This is a proper installable app, not just a bookmark. Once installed it opens full-screen with no browser chrome, gets its own icon, and works offline — the list, your ticks and every poster you have already seen are cached on your device.

Chrome, Edge or Brave on desktop: click the install icon in the address bar, or use the Install app button in the controls above.

Android (Chrome): tap the three-dot menu, then "Install app" or "Add to Home screen".

iPhone / iPad (Safari): tap the Share button, scroll down, tap "Add to Home Screen".

Your progress lives in the browser either way, so installing keeps everything you have ticked.

After Doomsday

What Marvel has actually announced for the other side of 18 December 2026, in the order it is currently expected to arrive. Brass dots are dates the studio has confirmed. Grey dots are either reported-but-not-official, or announced with no date at all — and anything undated sits below the dated films for the same year, because that is genuinely where it might land. Marvel has moved all of this before and will again.

4 June 2027 · Sony · not MCU

Spider-Man: Beyond the Spider-Verse

The third and final Miles Morales film, closing the animated trilogy. Dated, but outside MCU continuity entirely — it has no bearing on Secret Wars. If you tick the Spider-Verse extension above, this is where it finishes.

17 December 2027 · Cinemas

Avengers: Secret Wars

Anthony and Joe Russo again, exactly one year after Doomsday, closing the Multiverse Saga. Widely expected to reset the MCU — and, by most reporting, to recast several Infinity Saga leads on the way out. Whatever Doomsday breaks, this is where it gets rebuilt.

Reported 5 May 2028 · not yet official

Untitled X-Men film

Directed by Jake Schreier, who made Thunderbolts*. The first film after Secret Wars, with a recast X-Men team native to the rebuilt universe — not the Fox cast from Part V. This is the payoff the entire X-Men homework block is pointing at.

The 5 May 2028 date comes from trade reporting and has not been confirmed by Marvel or Disney. If it holds, it lands twenty years almost to the day after Iron Man.

15 December 2028 · Cinemas

Black Panther III

Announced at Comic-Con in July 2026 with a firm date. Ryan Coogler returns to direct a third and final time, and David Jonsson plays T'Challa's son taking up the mantle.

2028 · no date set

Ghost Rider

Announced at the same Comic-Con panel: Ryan Gosling as Johnny Blaze, directed by Shawn Levy. Marvel has slated it for 2028 but has not picked a date, which is why it sits below the December film rather than above it.

The first MCU Ghost Rider, arriving two decades after the Nicolas Cage films in Part IV.

Announced · no date

Blade

In development for years, through multiple writers and directors, without a release date. Mahershala Ali remains attached. Treat it as intended rather than imminent.

Announced · no date

Deadpool 4

Ryan Reynolds has confirmed he and Marvel Studios are working on it — his second Deadpool film inside the MCU. Nothing dated, nothing cast beyond the obvious.

Announced · no date

Armor Wars, Nova, and a Shang-Chi sequel

All three sit in Marvel's announced-but-undated pile. Armor Wars was originally a Disney+ series and was upgraded to a film; the Nova project has been in development since Phase 4; Shang-Chi's sequel was confirmed by Destin Daniel Cretton, who is now directing Spider-Man: Brand New Day first.

Beyond

Phase 7, whatever it turns out to be

Marvel has said the slate past Secret Wars is deliberately smaller and slower than the Phase 4 flood. Expect the map to be redrawn once Secret Wars has actually landed — which is the point of a film called Secret Wars.
